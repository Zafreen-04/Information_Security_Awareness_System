About the Information Security Awareness Measurement System

Comprehensive overview of the theoretical foundation, questionnaire design, psychometric metrics, and statistical methodology.

1. Project Purpose & Background

In educational ecosystems, high school students and teachers interact daily with internet resources, social platforms, and educational management portals. However, technical safeguards alone cannot mitigate threats stemming from human behaviour.

This project implements an interactive software system designed to operationalize the empirical research methodology outlined in the paper:
"Measuring information security behaviour of high school students and teachers". Rather than relying solely on abstract surveys, this platform collects real-time responses, stores data persistently in an SQLite database, calculates behavioral scores, executes psychometric validation tests, generates interactive dashboards, and synthesizes tailored remediation plans.

2. The Three Focus Areas & Questionnaire Items

The assessment investigates three primary human-centric cybersecurity vectors, evaluated on a standard 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree):

Code Focus Area Survey Statement
ISBP1 Password Management "I always use different passwords for each of my accounts."
ISBP2 Password Management "I never share my passwords with my friends or family."
ISBP3 Password Management "I always use a combination of numbers, letters and symbols to make a password."
ISBS1 Social Media Use "I always read the privacy settings of my social media or applications."
ISBS2 Social Media Use "I always think about the negative effect that might happen before posting something on my social media."
ISBS3 Social Media Use "I always filter what I would post on social media."
ISBE1 Email Use "I never click on suspicious links even if they are from people that I know."
ISBE2 Email Use "I never open a file that has been sent to me by people that I don't know."
ISBE3 Email Use "I never click on links that are sent to me from people that I don't know."

3. Scoring System & Classification Rubric

Likert answers (1 to 5) are converted to percentages using the formula:

percentage = (answer / 5) × 100

Focus area scores equal the arithmetic mean of their 3 constituent percentages. The composite overall score equals the mean of all 9 items. Scores are then mapped into the paper's three discrete categories:

Good
80% – 100%
High security awareness
Average
60% – 79%
Moderate awareness
Poor
0% – 59%
Low awareness, high vulnerability

4. Psychometric Validity & Reliability

In empirical social science and psychometrics, survey validity and reliability must be mathematically proven:

  • Pearson Item-Total Correlation (Validity): Evaluates whether each item correlates significantly with the overall score. The calculated correlation coefficient r must exceed the critical value r_crit derived from the two-tailed Student's t distribution with degrees of freedom df = N - 2 at α = 0.05.
  • Cronbach's Alpha (Reliability): Evaluates whether the set of items reliably measures a cohesive construct. Values above 0.70 demonstrate acceptable reliability, while values above 0.80 indicate good-to-excellent internal consistency.
